1Site and roof

The roof is a metal-sheet shed with a 60 m ridge and two 20 m slopes facing east and west at 7° tilt (DWG-01). After setbacks and two skylight strips per slope, 1,983 m² of the 2,400 m² roof is usable (83 %). All roof data are assumed; no site survey has been carried out.

Note 1, string length. The open-circuit voltage of a string rises as it gets cold. At the assumed record low of 3.4 °C, 18 modules would give 986.7 V, only 1.3 % under the 1,000 V connector limit, so 17 are used. The hot-cell maximum-power voltage with 17 modules is about 629 V, inside the 200–1,000 V tracking window.

Note 2, wind. Flush-mounted modules weigh about 0.12 kN/m². The governing checks are clamp pull-out and purlin strength, which need the actual roof and a structural engineer.

3Energy yield

PVsyst 7.4.7 gives 409,153 kWh a year (1,331 kWh/kWp, performance ratio 84.9 %). East–west orientation costs about 6 % against the best single tilt and spreads output across the day, which suits a two-shift factory.

4Grid connection

The factory takes power at 11 kV through its own transformer. WBSEDCL procedures allow an HT consumer to connect its plant at its low-voltage bus, provided the net meter is on the high-voltage side of the transformer.

Note 4, capacity cap. The utility document pairs 170 kW with 200 kVA, a power factor of about 0.85. If the cap means kWp at that ratio, a 350 kVA contract demand allows 297.5 kW, and this plant (307.4 kWp) would exceed it by 9.9 kWp; the alternative is 28 strings, 269 kWp. The documents do not say which reading applies. The larger size is retained and the question is open (Section 7).

5.1Basis

Grid price: WBSEDCL 11 kV industry Rate E(B), about ₹7.06 per kWh (V); the fuel-cost adjustment (MVCA) is charged on top and is not included. Capex: two installer pages (P). Maintenance, escalation and discount: assumed (A). Tax, loans and inverter replacement are not modelled, and the client is given no credit for lower demand charges (₹384 per kVA per month).

5.2Summary of results

At about ₹7 per kWh, a RESCO that discounts 20 to 30 % earns roughly 17 to 19 % a year on its capital (no tax or loan), and the factory saves ₹5 to ₹8 lakh in year 1 with no capital outlay. The largest risks are capex (installer pages, not a quotation), real yield (soiling and shading not yet modelled) and the reading of the capacity cap. If the factory is on the time-of-day scheme, the return is about 19.0 % instead of 19.4 %.

6Commissioning and safety

The repository holds a commissioning checklist with expected readings worked from the design: a healthy 17-module string should read about 882 V open-circuit at 25 °C cell temperature and 836 V at 45 °C (P). A one-page safety note covers falls through skylights, strings that cannot be switched off in daylight, and DC fire.

The test list follows an installer's summary of IEC 62446-1. The standard itself was not available to read, so acceptance ranges are common practice, not clauses.
